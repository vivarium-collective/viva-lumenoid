## At a glance

- **Scale:** ✅ **the fiber scale, for v1** — decided at the September 28th, 2026 Compositional modeling meeting. An explicit coarse-grained network (one collagen IV protomer = one two-bead rod, bead spacing ≈107–125 nm) in a flat periodic slab whose boundary stretches at a set rate. The vertex-scale form — an implicit graded field — is what v1 **produces**, not what it runs: its effective modulus and remodelling time become parameters the vertex model takes.
- **AICS model:** ❌ the 3D vertex model is still **not coupled to a basement membrane** (as of September 28th, 2026). The agreed route has two stages. **Offline first:** tabulate the BM's effective modulus and remodelling time against growth rate, and hand those to the vertex model. **Then direct:** vertex-mesh surface velocities drive the BM's substrate, BM stress is fed back, and the two exchange every N vertex steps because their timescales differ. Which vertex-model term receives the BM — a basal tension, a vertex mobility, or an elastic basal spring — is not yet fixed.

## v1 build — agreed September 28th, 2026

A standalone basement-membrane network on a moving boundary, run until it reports an elastic modulus and a remodelling time. This is the specification the meeting asked Matt to hand to Eran's Vivarium agents. Numbers and sources are on [the parameter sheet](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/yrDGpOUwz); what is decided and what is still open is in **v1 decisions**, below.

- **System — collagen IV, decided September 29th, 2026.** A flat, periodic slab of coarse-grained collagen IV, as in [@meadowcroft2025nonequilibrium](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/VddqScTYC): one protomer = one two-bead rod, the NC1 end binds 1 partner and the 7S end binds 3, and bonds form and break stochastically. **Code:** the LAMMPS input scripts released with [@barrientos2026basement](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/9vQd9sALc) — [github.com/Billie1717/BasementMembraneTurnoverSims](https://github.com/Billie1717/BasementMembraneTurnoverSims), archived as Zenodo [10.5281/zenodo.20719515](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20719515) (v1.0.0, CC-BY 4.0). Each stage folder (Assembly, Equilibration, BondSampling, Stretch, Growth) has a `build_*.py` that writes that stage's input script, `collagen.in`. Cloned at commit `ab4c004`; it runs locally.
- **Stage 1 — reproduce the paper, and separate elastic from viscous.** Stretch, hold, then switch the chemistry on. Reports the elastic response and the stress-relaxation time. A corrected, staged run started September 29th, 2026 — item 8 under **v1 decisions**.
- **Stage 2 — the growing substrate, flat.** [@barrientos2026basement](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/9vQd9sALc) grow the network at **fixed stress** and read off the growth rate. v1 inverts that: impose an **equibiaxial in-plane strain rate ε̇** and read off the stress. Result: σ(ε̇) — an effective viscosity η = σ/ε̇ at slow rates, and the rate at which the response turns elastic.
  - **Why flat is enough.** A patch of a sphere of radius R growing at Ṙ is stretched at ε̇ = Ṙ/R in every in-plane direction. So a flat, biaxially stretching slab **is** the growing shell to leading order, as long as R (tens of µm) is much larger than the mesh (~0.1 µm). ε̇ comes from the lumenoid radius time course — [the geometry request](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/4oqrBblCv) *(REQ candidate)*.
- **Stage 3 — add the cell–BM linker.** Linker sites tethered to one face of the slab, moving with the substrate; each forms and breaks a harmonic spring to a network bead. v1: uniform density, constant off-rate. Readouts: tension per linker and total traction. Spec under **has: adhesion**, below.
- **Stage 4 — curvature.** A true 3D shell, only if stages 2–3 give a result that depends on R beyond ε̇ = Ṙ/R.
- **Inputs:** protomer length 376 nm → bead spacing ≈107–125 nm · persistence length ≈40 nm · crosslink make/break (`MP`) · a source of new protomers · substrate strain rate ε̇ · linker density, stiffness and on/off rate. Values under **has: geometrical structure** and **has: adhesion**.
- **Readouts:** porosity (pore-size distribution) · extent of bundling · effective elastic modulus · remodelling time (the viscous part) · distribution of molecules at fluorescence resolution. Each is paired with its experimental comparator under **has: geometrical structure**.
- **Deferred to v2 and later:** linkers concentrated at the colony edge · force-dependent unbinding · a laminin network, alone or with collagen IV · crosslinking through other ECM molecules · tangential cell motion as a substrate velocity field · image → initial conditions · the direct multiscale loop.

## has: adhesion (integrin, ECM)

- **[CLM candidate]** [ECM is represented implicitly as a graded force field (vs explicit springs) for the post-formation regime](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/CCLVUug6j)
- **Spec:** 🟡 **the v1 linker is fixed in form, not in value** (September 28th, 2026). Form: tethered to the substrate, uniformly spaced, a harmonic spring, a constant off-rate, force read from the spring's extension. Values: the per-bond force scale (≈40 pN) and single-bond lifetime (≈46 s) come from the literature, but from other integrin–ligand pairs. Nothing is measured for α6β1–laminin, the pair hiPSCs use.
- ✅ **Filled since the 2026-08-10 gap was logged.** Strength: [40 pN per bond, with ≥4× subtype uncertainty](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/OcEhiFXdl) *(CLM candidate)* and [w_adh as an areal density of clustered bonds that switches on below ≈40 nm ligand spacing](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/kc5xEIVSi) *(CLM candidate)*. Spatial pattern: [w_adh varies ≈2.2× between colony edge and centre](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/zDUDWkrjl) *(CLM candidate)* — the colony-edge effect raised on September 28th, 2026, and already the evidence for the v2 linker distribution. Dynamics: [quasi-static, bond lifetime ≈46 s](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/UkjKJ_e4Z) *(CLM candidate)*. hPSC magnitude: [lower than any borrowed value](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/W83nwhJ5Z) *(CLM candidate)*.
- **Note:** the graph's existing integrin–ECM work sits in the formation project, not here; whether that evidence transfers to the post-formation regime is itself unexamined.
- **v1:** linkers tethered to the growing substrate and **uniformly** spaced; v2 follows integrin / focal-adhesion localisation, which is stronger at the colony edge. Decided September 28th, 2026; the capability request is **[REQ]** [include surface-attached fiber linkers](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/odeWtSI-D).
- **Which interfaces v1 covers.** Cell ↔ BM, through the linker, and BM cohesion, through the network. The BM ↔ coverslip interface on the adhesion board is out of v1: the lumenoid is modelled as a free shell growing inside its own BM.
- **Spreading or detachment? Neither.** Answers the adhesion board's open question. v1 imposes growth on the substrate and reads the force transmitted into the network — load transmission under a moving boundary. Equilibrium spreading and detachment under load remain later questions, each needing its own observable.
- **Linker mechanics for v1.** Springy, not inextensible: a force readout needs an extension to read (F = k·Δr), and the meeting asked for a spring-like attachment. Off-rate independent of force in v1: the 10–30 pN catch-bond window of [@kong2009demonstration](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/Y4mz9MtDH) is a v2 refinement, and the meeting judged force-sensitive unbinding unnecessary for the first runs.
- **The v1 linker stands in for a chain.** In a collagen-IV-only network, the "integrin" linker represents integrin → laminin → nidogen → collagen IV. Nidogen–laminin γ1 is nanomolar and effectively permanent ([K_d ≈ 1.1–1.4 nM](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/TTtjKJJAe)), so the chain's off-rate is set at the integrin–laminin end — which has no single-molecule measurement for α6β1–laminin-511. This is how the laminin evidence enters v1: through the linker's parameters, not as a network.
- **[CLM candidate]** The cell–BM linker turns over roughly a thousand times faster than the collagen IV crosslinks it anchors — about a minute against about a day — so over the network's remodelling time the substrate holds the network by friction rather than by permanent attachment
  - **Status:** 🟡 analogue-only — each end of the ratio is measured in a different system: the ≈46 s bond lifetime is a live-cell tension-sensor measurement, not on α6β1–laminin, and the ≈20 h collagen IV lifetime is from the Drosophila wing disc.
  - **Supported by**
    - [Integrin bond lifetime ≈46 s, so basal adhesion is quasi-static](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/UkjKJ_e4Z) *(CLM candidate)*
    - [Collagen IV mean lifetime ≈20 h in the Drosophila wing disc, by fluorescent timer](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/_cI9M02DZ) *(EVD candidate)*
  - **Why this ratio.** It is the one the room named: whether substrate motion realigns the network depends on "how strong integrins are attaching versus how strong the cross-linking is". At ~10³, single bonds cannot hold the network long enough to realign it. Clusters might — a focal adhesion holds many bonds in parallel and outlives any one of them — which is what Option B on the adhesion board computes.
  - **For the model:** set the linker off-rate as a multiple of the calibrated crosslink break rate, and sweep the multiple from ~10 to ~10³. Where network alignment appears is the prediction.
  - **[REQ candidate]** Measure integrin dwell times at the basal surface of hiPSC lumenoids by single-molecule tracking, because the linker-to-crosslink off-rate ratio decides whether substrate motion reorganises the basement membrane
  - **Informs:** **[QUE]** [What is the role of integrin-ECM attachments in lumenoid formation?](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/XIOkUhemm)

- **v1 inputs, with values** — the list written on the board on September 28th, 2026 (persistence length · average fiber length · creation of collagen fibers), plus the crosslink kinetics.
  - **Persistence length ≈40 nm** → **[CLM]** [Collagen IV is a semiflexible chain — persistence length ≈39 nm over a ≈360 nm contour](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/1MZ6CzCXM). **The two engines put this number in different places.** The LAMMPS rods are rigid, so flexibility enters only as soft angles where rods join — justified in the Meadowcroft supplement because the persistence length is shorter than a protomer. Smoldyn fibers bend along their length.
  - **Average fiber length 376 nm** → bead spacing a ≈ 107–125 nm: [the σ candidate](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/g360-n6WH) *(CLM candidate)* and [the supplement's own mapping, a ≈ 114 nm](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/C27Z8-CUn).
  - **Creation of collagen fibers** → the source term; see the two secretion blocks below.
  - **Crosslink on/off** → one knob. `MP` sets bond formation, and breaking is locked to it at `FactorMult` = 0.66. It is calibrated by matching the simulated relaxation time to [the ≈20 h collagen IV lifetime](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/_cI9M02DZ) *(EVD candidate)* — a target that spreads over 3–30 h across systems, and FRAP half-times measure mobility, not lifetime.
- **Bundling is a readout, not an input.** 5–7 protomers per strand (**[CLM]** [The collagen IV network's structural unit is a laterally bundled strand of 5–7 protomers, not a single protomer, so a model whose only bonds are NC1 and 7S omits the connectivity mode that carries most of the network's junctions](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/F1M-K968H)) is what the model should **produce**, from crosslink kinetics — the room's point that slow off-rates make bundles. The released model has no lateral-association bond, so it can bundle only through 7S branching. If it cannot reach 5–7, that is a named failure, and [the lateral-association candidate](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/abMjpmzjV) *(CLM candidate)* predicts an in-vitro-calibrated term would under-bundle about 2×.
- **Secretion adds whole protomers — collagen IV does not polymerise.** Answers "does collagen polymerize?" from the board. Three α chains assemble into a triple-helical protomer inside the cell, and that protomer is what is secreted. Outside, two protomers join through their NC1 ends into a hexamer, which peroxidasin welds with sulfilimine bonds; four join through their 7S ends, which lysyl oxidase-like 2 crosslinks. So the model's source term inserts **rods** — which is what the LAMMPS code already does — and the fibril-like strands the room described form outside the cell, by lateral association. Sources: [Cummings et al. 2016, J. Cell Biol. 213:479](https://rupress.org/jcb/article/213/4/479/38311/Extracellular-chloride-signals-collagen-IV-network) (chloride triggers NC1 hexamer assembly), [Basement membrane collagen IV: isolation of functional domains](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5828530/) (the assembly sequence), [LOXL2 cross-links collagen IV of glomerular BM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5207071/) (the 7S crosslink).
- **The LAMMPS model does add collagen — by concentration, not by rate.** Answers the meeting's open question; checked in the cloned code. Grand-canonical insertion and deletion (the "Nucleation" and "Death" reactions) hold free protomers at a target concentration, with a density penalty κ = 0.01. The insertion rate is therefore whatever keeps that concentration fixed — an **output**, not a secretion rate. [@barrientos2026basement](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/9vQd9sALc)'s growth runs use exactly this switch to separate "bond breakage, no new material" from "bond breakage + new material". Which to use in v1 is item 3 under **v1 decisions**.
- **Readouts, each paired with what it will be compared to**
  - **Porosity** → pore-size distribution. Comparators: 72 ± 40 nm pores on the exposed corneal BM ([@abrams2000nanoscale](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/Kd6Yyiprx), EM); a 20–60 nm hydrated Matrigel mesh.
  - **Bundling** → protomers per strand. Comparator: 5–7 in the PFHR-9 cell-deposited scaffold ([@pokidysheva2025targeted](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/0SdwVIqBW)).
  - **Effective elastic modulus.** Comparators: shear modulus 1025 ± 496 Pa for an MCF10A acinus BM ([@fabris2018nanoscale](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/V6IYVYVpD), AFM) against 82 kPa for an MDA-MB-231 spheroid BM ([@li2021nonlinear](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/V6mMFDr-G), micro-inflation). **Not reportable in pascals until the energy scale is fixed** — item 4 under **v1 decisions**; the published model's own linear modulus is ≈0.03 Pa.
  - **Remodelling time** → the viscous part. Comparator: collagen IV lifetime ≈20 h. This is also what calibrates `MP`, so it cannot also validate the model.
  - **Distribution of molecules** → AICS immunofluorescence. ⚠️ AICS images **laminin**, so a collagen IV v1 can be compared to it only through a proxy.
- **"What's the distribution of pore sizes?" — nanoscale and fluorescence-scale are two different readouts, and the model can give both.** Pores (≈70 nm) are far below optical resolution (≈250 nm), so fluorescence cannot see them; it sees density texture — the thin regions and holes the room pointed at. **Nanoscale:** measure pore sizes directly on the simulated network and compare to EM, then to cryo-ET when [the request](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/uvg7sxAjM) *(REQ candidate)* is met. **Fluorescence-scale:** blur the simulated density with the microscope's point-spread function and compare the texture to AICS images.

What still separates them: LAMMPS runs today with the published parameters; Smoldyn has flexible fibers and gets surface binders first (top of Steve's list), but needs its unmerged development branch packaged for Vivarium. **Settled by** which engine Eran's agents will run, and when Steve's surface binders land. Recommendation: LAMMPS for stages 1–2 now; decide at stage 3.
  - **3 · Secretion — fixed concentration or fixed flux?** The published model holds concentration fixed, so a growing substrate can never thin its BM. A fixed secretion flux per unit area lets the BM thin when growth outpaces secretion — the regime the adhesion board's percolation prediction is about. **Settled by** whether v1 asks "how does the BM respond to growth" (concentration) or "can secretion keep up with growth" (flux). Recommendation: concentration in stage 1, to reproduce the paper; flux in stage 2.
  - **4 · Energy scale — can the modulus be reported in pascals?** The published model answers half of this itself: the Meadowcroft supplement reports the network's own linear Young's modulus as **≈0.03 Pa**, against 50–500 Pa for reconstituted BM, and attributes the gap to missing components, thin-sheet geometry, absent crosslinkers and laminin, and no pre-stress ([read from the supplement, AllenCell §9](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AllenCell/page/FN5xU0I_M)). That fits one energy unit being the thermal energy kT, which makes one stress unit kT/a³ ≈ 2–3 Pa at a ≈ 114–125 nm. So with the published units, v1's moduli will come out 10³–10⁴× softer than reconstituted BM. **The choice:** fit the energy scale to a measured modulus — then the modulus is an input, and only ratios and the relaxation time are predictions — or keep kT and report stiffness only as a ratio, as the paper's phase diagram does with `s_E`. **Settled by** what the coupling needs (item 5).
  - **5 · The coupling contract — which vertex-model term receives the BM?** The meeting named vertex mobility. The options are a basal surface tension, a basal friction (mobility), or an elastic basal spring, and the choice decides whether v1 must report a viscosity, a modulus, or both. **Settled by** what Ehssan's and Sassan's vertex model can take.
  - **6 · Linker values — density, spring constant, off-rate.** One coarse linker is not one integrin: at the ≈40 nm clustering spacing, a ~120 nm bead patch holds ~9 ligand sites, so a coarse linker is a small cluster. Recommendation: set the off-rate as a multiple of the crosslink break rate and sweep it ([the ratio claim](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AICS-Lumenoids/page/5wvXLr66j) *(CLM candidate)*); sweep density as a dimensionless coverage.

Until then, sweep ε̇ across a range that brackets the calibrated remodelling time.
  - **8 · The Stage 1 stretch test — what went wrong, and the corrected run.** Inserting protomers during the stretch is **by design**: the paper's Fig. 2B.3 shows that a network without new material cannot expand intact. Our earlier runs failed for four other reasons, found on September 14th, 2026 ([AllenCell record](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/AllenCell/page/FN5xU0I_M)): `Nevery` 500 instead of the paper's 5000; a relaxation window 8× too short; an equilibration 59× too short; and reading the all-atom stress instead of the network stress normalised by the initial volume. One more surfaced on September 29th, 2026: in the released Stretch input script crosslinks cannot form or break during the ramp — only insertion runs — so part of the connectivity collapse recorded on September 14 may be dilution, thousands of inserted free protomers enlarging the denominator of percent-in-largest-cluster, rather than fragmentation. The new analysis counts network protomers instead. **A corrected, staged run started September 29th, 2026**: seven arms from one starting network (`~/Repos/allencell-helper/external/stretch-staged/`). **Still open:** the 59×-short equilibration — an overnight run.
